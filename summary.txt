World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia and Ukraine ended two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, their first in nine months, agreeing a prisoner exchange but no ceasefire, with the Donbas still deadlocked (Reuters). Jared Kushner said there was "no trust" between the sides; he and Steve Witkoff go to Moscow on Thursday to meet Vladimir Putin, then Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Ireland. Russia struck six electricity substations in central Ukraine; a market attack in the Odesa region killed three (Reuters).

At COP31 in Antalya, leaders began two days of speeches, with Antonio Guterres warning the climate crisis was "outrunning us" on a "path to mutually assured destruction" (Reuters). Turkey seeks a goal of 35% electricity in energy use by 2035; the US skipped the talks for a second year.

Iran designated Argentina's navy and air force as terrorist organisations, and security chief Mohsen Rezaei warned of attacks on distant US bases if the US struck again (Reuters). UK Maritime Trade Operations confirmed a projectile struck a tanker off Oman on Tuesday; Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed three tankers hit mines, unverified (UKMTO, Reuters). Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved onto a military base amid Iranian threats, sources told the New York Times.

Israeli strikes killed three Palestinians in Gaza, including two brothers in a tent for displaced people, medics said; Israel said it struck militants (Reuters).

NATO scrambled jets after at least four drones strayed into Baltic airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia, prompting shelter alerts in Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia; none were injured (Reuters).

British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army soldier near Gloucester on Tuesday on suspicion of spying for Russia; he served with NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps and was uncharged (BBC).

A CloudSek report said a Kazakhstan-linked group used an AI agent framework to steal over 2,000 national-security documents from Tajikistan (Reuters).
